# V3.1-ARCH-06: Evidence and Gate Contracts

**Status:** Proposed architecture baseline  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-09-16  
**Depends on:** V3.1-ARCH-01 through V3.1-ARCH-05

## 1. Purpose

This specification defines what V3-AIDLC accepts as proof and how that proof controls advancement.

In plain terms, an agent saying “I finished” is not enough. The framework must know what was checked, which exact version was checked, who or what performed the check, whether the proof is still current, whether an independent reviewer was actually independent, and whether every required check passed.

The model separates three concepts:

- **Evidence:** the proof itself, such as a test report, review disposition, scan, artifact, observation, or metric.
- **Check result:** a bounded conclusion produced by applying one Check Contract to admissible evidence.
- **Gate result:** a decision about whether the required collection of checks permits a specific transition.

## 2. Evidence is not a claim

An evidence record contains:

- stable evidence ID and kind;
- exact subject ID and revision;
- producer identity, role, and independence group;
- collection timestamp;
- durable locator;
- SHA-256 digest;
- explicit assertions supported by the evidence;
- and optional superseded evidence IDs.

The digest makes accidental or silent substitution detectable. The subject revision prevents a passing result from an older build being applied to a newer one.

Evidence may support a claim without proving every conclusion an agent draws from it. Check Contracts define the allowed interpretation.

## 3. Evidence kinds

The bootstrap vocabulary includes:

| Kind | Typical meaning |
|---|---|
| `artifact` | A built or authored deliverable |
| `test_result` | Output from an automated or manual test procedure |
| `build_result` | Reproducible build or packaging output |
| `review_disposition` | A reviewer’s structured acceptance or findings |
| `scan_result` | Security, dependency, quality, or compliance scan |
| `observation` | Recorded real-world or user observation |
| `metric` | Measured performance or operational value |
| `decision` | A recorded human or governed decision |
| `provenance_manifest` | A trace of inputs, tools, versions, and producers |

New kinds require a versioned schema change. Free-form labels cannot silently acquire governed meaning.

## 4. Admissibility

Evidence is admissible for a Check only when it:

- validates against the evidence schema;
- has a kind accepted by the Check Contract;
- is bound to the Check result’s exact subject and revision;
- includes the required tamper-evident digest;
- is not dated after the Check evaluation;
- is no older than the Check’s freshness limit;
- satisfies the minimum evidence count;
- and is available through its recorded locator.

Missing, malformed, stale, future-dated, wrong-subject, or wrong-revision evidence is not treated as a failed product. It is an evidence failure, producing an inconclusive readiness result until the proof is recollected or the discrepancy is investigated.

## 5. Check Contracts

A Check Contract defines:

- stable ID and semantic version;
- title and evaluation method;
- accepted evidence kinds;
- minimum evidence count;
- freshness limit;
- digest requirement;
- independence requirement;
- and the semantics of pass, fail, inconclusive, and error.

Initial methods are:

- `deterministic`: reproducible rules or tools produce the result;
- `semantic_review`: an authorized reviewer applies explicit criteria;
- `human_verification`: an accountable person confirms a condition that cannot be reliably automated.

A Check result records the exact Check Contract version, subject revision, evaluator, evaluation time, evidence IDs, subject-producer independence group, and declared status.

## 6. Check statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `passed` | The criteria were evaluated and satisfied. |
| `failed` | The criteria were evaluated and not satisfied. |
| `inconclusive` | Available proof cannot support a reliable pass or failure. |
| `error` | The Check itself could not complete reliably. |

A declared status only governs when its evidence is admissible. A `passed` label with stale or mismatched evidence does not pass a Gate.

## 7. Independence

When a Check requires independent evaluation, the evaluator’s independence group must differ from the subject producer’s group.

An independence group represents shared control or incentive, not merely a different agent-run ID. Spawning the same role again with the same context and authority does not automatically create independence.

Independent reviewers receive the evidence and criteria needed to judge the result, but cannot modify the candidate they are reviewing. Repair returns to an implementation role and requires new evidence.

## 8. Gate Contracts

Gate types reflect different transition scopes:

- Package Gate: accept one Work Package.
- Bolt Gate: integrate a bounded delivery cycle.
- Stage Gate: move a Unit to another lifecycle Stage.
- Integration Gate: combine parallel work safely.
- Promotion Gate: move a candidate to a higher-consequence environment.
- Release Gate: declare a governed release outcome.

A Gate Contract defines required, conditional, and advisory Check rules. Conditional Checks use the same constrained `equals` and `in` predicates as Route selection. Arbitrary scripts and model-authored executable expressions are not permitted.

## 9. Gate outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning | May advance? |
|---|---|---|
| `passed` | All selected required Checks passed with admissible evidence. | Yes, subject to authorization policy. |
| `passed_with_exception` | Required Checks passed or have valid bounded exceptions. | Yes, subject to authorization and exception conditions. |
| `failed` | At least one required, admissible Check explicitly failed without a valid exception. | No. |
| `inconclusive` | A required result or admissible proof is missing, invalid, errored, or inconclusive. | No. |

Gate pass and authorization are separate. A passed Production Promotion Gate establishes readiness; ARCH-04 still determines whether the Project Lead may perform the promotion automatically or must return to the user.

## 10. Deterministic evaluation

The Gate Engine:

1. loads and validates the versioned Gate and referenced Check Contracts;
2. selects required and matching conditional Checks from authoritative facts;
3. records advisory Checks separately;
4. rejects duplicate Evidence IDs, Check results, or Check exceptions;
5. verifies every result targets the Gate’s exact subject revision;
6. evaluates evidence admissibility and independence;
7. treats missing or invalid proof as inconclusive;
8. treats admissible failed Checks as failures;
9. evaluates bounded exceptions only for explicitly waivable Checks;
10. emits outcome, promotability, required Checks, missing Checks, invalid Checks, accepted exceptions, and reasons.

For the same contract versions, facts, subject revision, Check results, evidence, exceptions, and evaluation time, the result is deterministic.

## 11. Exceptions

An exception is not hidden skipping. It is a visible, bounded acceptance of a known failed or incomplete Check.

A valid exception records:

- stable exception ID;
- exact Gate and Check;
- exact subject and revision;
- accepted authority type;
- accountable grantor;
- rationale and compensating control when applicable;
- expiration time;
- and the Decision or event that created it.

The Gate rule must explicitly mark the Check as waivable, and the authority must be accepted by the Gate Contract. Hard-control Checks are non-waivable by construction. An expired, mismatched, incomplete, or unauthorized exception is rejected.

Exceptions do not rewrite the Check to `passed`; the Gate outcome remains visibly `passed_with_exception`.

## 12. Supersession and freshness

Evidence is append-only. New evidence may supersede older evidence, but history remains attributable.

Any material subject change creates a new revision and invalidates prior revision-bound proof for promotion purposes. Contracts define freshness in seconds because acceptable age varies by Check and target:

- a design decision may remain relevant for weeks;
- an automated test may need to run on every candidate revision;
- an operational health check may be valid for minutes;
- a security scan may expire when dependencies or configuration change.

Changing a timestamp or copying an old report does not refresh evidence. A new collection event and digest are required.

## 13. Findings and repair

A failed Check should emit typed findings that identify severity, affected subject, evidence, and recommended disposition. Findings may lead to:

- bounded repair;
- replanning;
- a product decision;
- accepted negative learning;
- or a proposed exception when policy allows it.

After repair, the changed subject receives a new revision. Required Checks run again against new evidence. The previous failed result remains part of the audit history.

## 14. Negative and partial results

Evidence can validly prove that an approach does not work. Feasibility and investigation Routes may treat well-supported negative learning as a successful completion outcome.

This does not mean a failed production Check becomes a pass. Route completion semantics determine whether the goal was to deliver a working capability or reliably answer a question.

Partial success is recorded at the smallest truthful scope. Passing one Package Gate does not imply its Bolt, Stage, Integration, Promotion, or Release Gate passed.

## 15. Liaison behavior

The liaison translates Gate results into ordinary language:

- what was checked;
- what passed;
- what failed or remains unproven;
- whether the issue is with the product or the proof;
- what can continue;
- and what action or decision is needed.

It should say “the latest build has not yet been proven safe to release” rather than exposing internal Gate status names unless the user asks for technical details.

The liaison does not characterize an inconclusive result as a failure, present a `passed_with_exception` result as an unqualified pass, or claim completion without a Project Lead receipt.

## 16. Bootstrap implementation

The initial implementation includes:

- JSON schemas for Evidence Records, Check Contracts, and Gate Contracts;
- deterministic evidence and contract validators;
- automated-test, independent-review, and security-scan Check Contracts;
- a representative Production Promotion Gate;
- evidence freshness, revision binding, and digest enforcement;
- evaluator independence checks;
- conditional security selection;
- bounded exception validation;
- and deterministic Gate outcome tests.

## 17. Acceptance criteria

V3.1-ARCH-06 is acceptable when:

1. Evidence, Check results, and Gate results are distinct governed records.
2. Evidence is bound to an exact subject revision and producer.
3. Missing or inadmissible evidence cannot produce a pass.
4. Evidence failures remain distinct from product failures.
5. Independent review cannot be satisfied by the subject producer’s independence group.
6. Required, conditional, and advisory Checks are unambiguous.
7. Gate outcomes distinguish pass, pass with exception, failure, and inconclusive proof.
8. Hard-control Checks cannot be waived.
9. Waivable exceptions are exact, authorized, attributable, and expiring.
10. Gate pass does not bypass separate authorization policy.
11. Negative learning can close learning-focused Routes without weakening delivery Gates.
12. Representative contracts and deterministic tests pass.
